Red Rocks Park & Amphitheatre

Red Rocks is a Denver city park first and a world-famous concert venue second. You can walk into the amphitheatre, hike between sandstone monoliths and watch the sun rise over the plains without a ticket, as long as you know how event days work.

Most people first see Red Rocks on a concert poster, but the park around the stage is just as remarkable in daylight. Giant tilted slabs of red sandstone rise out of the hogback west of Denver, and the amphitheatre sits between two of them in a natural bowl with near-perfect acoustics. The City and County of Denver owns the park as part of its Denver Mountain Parks system, which is why visiting it outside of a show costs nothing.

Understanding event days

This is the single most important thing to know. Red Rocks books a long season of concerts, films and community events, and on those days the public loses access to the amphitheatre in the early afternoon so crews can set up and sound check.

  • Non-event daysThe park, trails and amphitheatre are open from one hour before sunrise to one hour after sunset.
  • Event daysThe park opens one hour before sunrise, and public access to the venue generally ends around 2 p.m.
  • Exact timesVenue closure times are set the week of each event and can change at any time.
  • Check the same dayThe official closure line is 720-865-2494, and the event calendar is on the official site.

The practical rule: if you want to walk into the amphitheatre during a busy concert month, go in the morning. Sunrise visits are both the most beautiful and the most reliable.

What to do on a daytime visit

Walk into the amphitheatre

Climb the long staircases from the lower lots or enter from the top plaza. Standing on the stage looking up at the rows of benches, framed by the two giant monoliths, is the classic Red Rocks experience. Early mornings bring a steady stream of locals running the stairs and doing workouts on the rows. Be courteous, keep to the sides if you are moving slowly and follow any posted rules.

Hike the Trading Post Trail

The 1.4-mile Trading Post Trail starts and ends at the historic Trading Post building and loops through the rock formations south of the amphitheatre, with some steps and modest climbing. It is the best short walk in the park and suits most ages. Watch for rattlesnakes on warm days and stay on the trail; the sandstone erodes easily.

Other trails

A short geologic overlook trail near the upper parking area gives a quick view over the formations. The longer Red Rocks Trail links the park with the surrounding foothills and with Jefferson County open space trails to the north and south, for those who want more distance.

History and the Visitor Center

The amphitheatre was built by the Civilian Conservation Corps and Works Progress Administration in the late 1930s and opened in 1941. Its long history of performances is part of the Colorado Music Hall of Fame exhibits near the Trading Post. The Visitor Center at the top of the venue normally has displays on the geology and construction, but the official site has posted a construction closure notice, so confirm before you go.

The geology in two minutes

The red rocks are part of the Fountain Formation, sandstone and conglomerate made from sediment that washed off an ancient mountain range. Much later, as the modern Rockies rose, those flat layers were tilted upward along the edge of the range. The same uplift created the Flatirons in Boulder and the formations at Garden of the Gods, which is why they look like relatives. Just east of the park, the younger rocks of Dinosaur Ridge hold well-known dinosaur tracks and bones.

Dinosaur Ridge, next door

Across the road from the park's north entrance area, Dinosaur Ridge is a National Natural Landmark with a paved road along the ridge where you can see dinosaur footprints preserved in sandstone and exposed bones in the rock. A nonprofit runs a visitor center with exhibits, and guided options run on a schedule. It pairs naturally with a morning at Red Rocks, especially for children.

Parking and getting there

Red Rocks is about 15 miles west of downtown Denver. Most drivers take I-70 west and exit for Morrison, or approach from the south along CO-93. On non-event days there are several free lots, including the Upper North, Lower North, Upper South and Lower South lots and the Top Circle near the main entrance. The lower lots involve a steep climb to the venue, which is a real effort at about 6,400 feet, so allow time and carry water.

The town of Morrison, just south of the park, has a short main street with restaurants and is a pleasant place for breakfast after a sunrise visit.

Going to a show

Seeing a concert here is a bucket-list experience, and a little planning makes the night better.

  1. Buy tickets from the official site or its listed ticketing partners to avoid counterfeits.
  2. Know your lot. Lots generally open a couple of hours before doors. Your ticket or the venue's guidance may specify where to park.
  3. Dress for two climates. A warm afternoon in the parking lot can turn into a cold night in the stands once the sun drops behind the mountains.
  4. Read the bag policy on the official site before you go; it changes and is strictly enforced.
  5. Plan the ride home. Rideshare pickup areas are crowded after shows, and cell service can be slow. Shuttle services and a designated driver are both popular choices.

Summer thunderstorms are common in the foothills. Lightning can pause or delay shows, and the venue is open-air. Watch the official channels for weather holds, and never shelter under the rock formations during a storm.

Free summer events and fitness

Beyond concerts, Red Rocks hosts long-running seasonal programs such as film nights and sunrise yoga sessions, which are ticketed or require registration. The annual Easter Sunrise Service is a decades-old tradition. Dates and formats are announced each spring on the official site.

Frequently asked questions

Can you visit Red Rocks without a concert ticket?

Yes. Red Rocks Park is open to the public on non-event days from one hour before sunrise to one hour after sunset, and the amphitheatre, trails and grounds can be visited for free. On event days the venue closes to the public in the early afternoon, around 2 p.m., and exact times are set the week of the show.

How do I find out if Red Rocks is closed today?

The official Red Rocks website lists the event calendar, and its same-day closure line, 720-865-2494, gives current access times. Closure times can change at short notice.

How long is the Trading Post Trail?

The Trading Post Trail is a 1.4-mile loop that begins and ends at the Trading Post building. It follows the same hours as the park and closes early on event days.

Is the Red Rocks Visitor Center open?

The official site has posted a notice that the Visitor Center is undergoing construction and closed until further notice, alongside its usual seasonal hours. Check the Visitor Center page on the official site before planning around it.

How do you get to Red Rocks without a car?

Public transit does not serve the venue directly. On concert nights many people use private shuttle services or rideshare; plan your return ride before the show, as pickup areas get very busy after the encore.
