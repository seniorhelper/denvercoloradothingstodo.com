Denver in Winter

Denver winters surprise visitors. Snowstorms arrive, but sunny days often follow within a day or two, and the city stays busy with holiday lights, a two-week livestock show and easy access to the ski areas.

Winter in Denver is not the long gray season many visitors expect. The city sits on the high plains in the rain shadow of the Rockies, so storms that bury the mountains often bring only a few inches downtown, and the strong sun that follows can melt it remarkably fast. Expect cold mornings, some genuinely snowy days, plenty of blue sky and a busy calendar from Thanksgiving through the first weeks of spring.

What the weather is really like

  • Snow patternStorms come and go. A snowy morning can be followed by a dry, sunny afternoon, and many storms melt from roads within a day or two.
  • Snowiest monthMarch often brings Denver's heaviest, wettest snows, sometimes alongside warm, springlike days in the same week.
  • Big swingsDaily temperature ranges are wide. A frosty dawn can turn into a sunny afternoon warm enough for a light jacket.
  • Mountains differThe ski towns west of Denver are colder and much snowier, with snow on the ground all season.

The National Weather Service office in Boulder issues Denver's forecasts and winter storm warnings. Check it before driving anywhere.

Holiday season highlights

Zoo Lights

The Denver Zoo's long-running Zoo Lights fills the City Park campus with illuminated animal sculptures and light displays on selected evenings from late November or December into early January. Tickets are separate from daytime admission and sell out on popular nights.

Blossoms of Light

The Denver Botanic Gardens on York Street turns its gardens into a walk-through light display in the holiday season, with timed tickets. Bundle up; it is outdoors.

Downtown lights and markets

Downtown traditionally hosts a holiday parade of lights in early December, and the City and County Building at Civic Center is famously covered in colored lights for the season. Union Station and Civic Center have hosted holiday markets with local vendors. Exact dates and formats change; check organizers' sites each year.

January: the National Western Stock Show

Every January since the early 1900s, the National Western Stock Show has filled the grounds north of downtown with rodeos, livestock competitions, horse shows and a huge trade show. It runs for a little over two weeks and is the most Western event of the Denver year. The traditional kickoff includes a cattle drive along 17th Street downtown. Rodeo tickets for prime sessions sell early.

Indoor Denver

Cold days are museum days. The Golden Triangle museums sit within a few blocks of each other, so you can move between the Denver Art Museum, the Clyfford Still Museum and History Colorado with only short walks in the cold. The Museum of Nature & Science, the Downtown Aquarium, Meow Wolf Convergence Station and the Denver Performing Arts Complex's theater season round out the options. Denver Union Station's Great Hall is a warm, handsome place to sit with a coffee between activities.

Seasonal outdoor rinks have operated downtown in winter, and the frozen surface of Evergreen Lake in the foothills, about 30 to 40 minutes west, has been a classic skating spot when conditions allow. Operators set opening dates based on weather.

Ski and snow day trips

Denver is a practical base for skiing without staying in a resort town. The closest larger ski areas are roughly one to two hours west along I-70 and US-40, and small areas sit closer still.

The ski train

Amtrak's Winter Park Express has run from Denver Union Station to the base of Winter Park Resort, about 70 miles by rail through the Moffat Tunnel. In the 2025 to 2026 season it left Union Station in the morning and returned in the evening on weekends and selected weekdays from mid-December through late March, with skis and snowboards carried at no extra charge. Amtrak announced that the train will return for 2026 to 2027; watch its site for the schedule.

By bus

The Colorado Department of Transportation has run seasonal Snowstang buses from Denver to several ski areas, and its year-round Bustang and Pegasus services follow the I-70 corridor. These avoid winter driving altogether.

By car

  1. Know the traction law. From September through May, Colorado can activate its passenger vehicle traction law on I-70 in the mountains. You need suitable tires with adequate tread, four-wheel or all-wheel drive, or approved traction devices.
  2. Check COtrip. Closures for crashes, avalanche control and storms are common. CDOT's COtrip site shows live conditions and alerts.
  3. Leave very early on weekends and return before or after the afternoon rush, especially on Sundays and holiday Mondays.
  4. Pack for delays. Water, snacks, warm clothes, a charged phone and a full tank or charge make a long closure tolerable.

Lower-elevation winter outings

Not every winter trip needs a mountain pass. Garden of the Gods in Colorado Springs, Red Rocks and Golden are much lower and often snow-free between storms. The Rocky Mountain Arsenal wildlife refuge is a good winter choice too; bald eagles have been seen there in the colder months. Snowy days at City Park and Washington Park bring sledding hills and quiet, beautiful walks.

What to pack

  • A warm, windproof coat plus a lighter layer for sunny afternoons
  • Hat, gloves and a scarf
  • Waterproof shoes or boots for slush
  • Sunglasses and sunscreen, because snow and altitude intensify UV
  • Lip balm and moisturizer for the very dry air

Frequently asked questions

Does it snow a lot in Denver?

Denver gets several snowstorms each winter, and March is often its snowiest month. But the dry air, strong sun and frequent warm spells mean snow in the city usually melts within days. The mountains to the west get far more and keep it all season.

Is Denver worth visiting in winter?

Yes, especially for travelers who combine the city with ski days. Hotel prices are often lower outside holiday weeks, museums are quieter, and December and January bring holiday lights and the National Western Stock Show.

Can you take a train from Denver to a ski resort?

Amtrak's Winter Park Express has run seasonal ski trains from Denver Union Station to Winter Park Resort. Amtrak said the train would return for the 2026 to 2027 season; check Amtrak's site for dates and fares once they are published.

What should I wear in Denver in winter?

Layers. Days can swing from below freezing in the morning to the 40s or 50s by afternoon in sun. Bring a warm coat, hat and gloves, waterproof shoes for slushy sidewalks, sunglasses and sunscreen.
