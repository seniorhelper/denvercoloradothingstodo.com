Rocky Mountain National Park From Denver

Rocky Mountain National Park is the big one: alpine tundra, glacier-carved lakes and herds of elk, about two hours from downtown Denver. A day trip works, but only if you understand the timed entry system and leave early.

Rocky Mountain National Park protects more than 400 square miles of the Front Range, straddling the Continental Divide northwest of Denver. Its most famous road, Trail Ridge Road, climbs above 12,000 feet and crosses miles of alpine tundra. The park is one of the most visited in the country, which is why it manages summer and early-fall traffic with timed entry reservations. With a reservation, an early start and a realistic plan, it makes a spectacular single day from Denver.

Timed entry, explained simply

The park's timed entry system changes a little each year. In 2026 it worked like this, according to the National Park Service:

  • SeasonFrom Friday, May 22 into mid-October 2026. The NPS announcement and the reservation page listed slightly different end dates, so confirm on Recreation.gov.
  • Most of the parkA reservation was needed to enter between 9 a.m. and 2 p.m.
  • Bear Lake Road corridorA separate "Timed Entry + Bear Lake Road" reservation covered 5 a.m. to 6 p.m.
  • Release scheduleBlocks released on the 1st of each month at 8 a.m. MT, plus a nightly release at 7 p.m. for the next day.
  • Per vehicleOne reservation per vehicle per day, non-transferable, with a small reservation fee.
  • Still requiredA valid park entrance pass, such as a day or weekly pass or an America the Beautiful annual pass.

Two ways around the permit: arrive before the reserved window starts, or after it ends. Entering the main park before 9 a.m. avoided the need for a timed entry reservation in 2026 (Bear Lake Road kept its own, longer window), and it is also the best time for wildlife and parking. Campground and wilderness reservations include entry in their own ways; see the park camping guide on our companion site.

Getting there from Denver

Most visitors enter on the east side through Estes Park. There are two main routes:

Routes from Denver to the east side of the park
RouteHow it goesWhy choose it
US-36 via Boulder and LyonsNorth through Boulder, then up the canyon from Lyons to Estes ParkUsually the quickest from central Denver; scenic canyon finish
I-25 and US-34 via LovelandNorth on I-25, then west up the Big Thompson CanyonGood from north Denver suburbs; dramatic narrow canyon
CO-7 or CO-72 Peak to PeakVia Nederland or Lyons along the foothillsSlower but beautiful, especially with fall aspens

The west entrance near Grand Lake is a different trip entirely, reached over Berthoud Pass via I-70 and US-40 or by crossing Trail Ridge Road from the east. For a single day from Denver, stick to the east side.

What to do with one day

Bear Lake Road

The Bear Lake corridor reaches the park's most popular trailheads. Bear Lake itself has a flat loop of about half a mile with views of Hallett Peak. From the same trailhead, a short climb leads to Nymph, Dream and Emerald lakes, a classic morning hike of a few miles round trip. A park shuttle has run from a park-and-ride lot along Bear Lake Road in summer, which helps when parking fills, often very early.

Trail Ridge Road

Trail Ridge Road links Estes Park and Grand Lake and tops out above 12,000 feet. Pullouts such as Many Parks Curve, Rainbow Curve and the Forest Canyon Overlook give huge views, and the Tundra Communities Trail is a short paved walk across the tundra. The Alpine Visitor Center near Fall River Pass has exhibits and restrooms in season. Allow several hours for a round trip to the visitor center with stops.

Wildlife meadows

Moraine Park and Horseshoe Park are broad meadows where elk graze, especially early and late in the day. In September and early October, the elk rut fills the evenings with bugling. Bighorn sheep are often seen near Sheep Lakes in Horseshoe Park in early summer. Keep at least 25 yards from elk and other large animals and use pullouts rather than stopping in traffic.

Old Fall River Road

This one-way gravel road climbs from Horseshoe Park to the Alpine Visitor Center and is open only for part of the summer. It is slow and narrow, with no guardrails, and offers a quieter climb than Trail Ridge.

A sample day

  1. 5:30 a.m. Leave Denver with breakfast and water packed.
  2. 7:30 a.m. Enter on the east side. With a Timed Entry + Bear Lake Road reservation, drive Bear Lake Road and hike to Dream or Emerald Lake; without one, start with a walk in Moraine Park instead.
  3. 11:30 a.m. Picnic in the Moraine Park area.
  4. 12:30 p.m. Drive Trail Ridge Road to the Alpine Visitor Center, stopping at overlooks. Watch for building storms.
  5. 4:00 p.m. Back down to Horseshoe Park for wildlife, then into Estes Park for dinner.
  6. 6:30 p.m. Head home to Denver, arriving around 8:30 p.m.

Safety and comfort

  • Lightning: Trail Ridge is fully exposed above treeline. If you hear thunder, return to your vehicle and drive lower.
  • Altitude: Much of Trail Ridge sits above 11,000 feet. Visitors fresh from sea level should go easy and drink water.
  • Cold: Temperatures on the tundra can be far colder than in Estes Park. Bring a warm layer and a rain shell.
  • Cell service: Patchy inside the park. Download maps and your reservation confirmation before you go.
  • Road status: The park lists road conditions on its website, and its contacts page lists a recorded Trail Ridge Road line.

After timed entry ends in October and before Trail Ridge closes for winter, the park is quieter and the light is beautiful, but storms can close high roads with little notice. In winter the lower valleys stay open for snowshoeing and wildlife watching.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a reservation to enter Rocky Mountain National Park?

During the timed entry season, yes, unless you arrive outside the reserved hours. In 2026, timed entry reservations were required from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. daily from May 22 into mid-October for most of the park, and from 5 a.m. to 6 p.m. for the Bear Lake Road corridor into later October. One reservation covers one vehicle, and an entrance pass is still required.

When do timed entry reservations become available?

In 2026, the park released reservations on the first of each month at 8 a.m. Mountain Time for the following month's dates, and released an additional batch at 7 p.m. the evening before each day. Both sold out quickly.

Is Trail Ridge Road open in October?

Trail Ridge Road, which crosses the Continental Divide, typically closes for the winter season in mid to late October once snow arrives, and reopens around Memorial Day. Closures can come earlier with storms. Check the park's road status line or website on the day.

Can you see elk in Rocky Mountain National Park?

Yes, very often. Elk graze in the meadows of Moraine Park, Horseshoe Park and the Kawuneeche Valley. September and early October is the rut, when bulls bugle, which draws large crowds at dawn and dusk. Stay at least 25 yards away and never surround an animal.
