Day Trips From Denver

Six day trips that work from a Denver base, compared honestly: how long the drive really takes, which ones need a reservation before you leave, and which months each one is at its best.

Denver's great advantage as a base is that the plains end and the mountains begin almost at the city limits. Within an hour of downtown you can stand in a Victorian mining town, walk under the Flatirons or watch bighorn sheep on a cliff above the interstate. Within two hours you can be on the alpine tundra of a national park. The trick is matching the trip to the season, the day of the week and the amount of driving you can actually enjoy.

This page compares the six day trips we cover in depth. Each one links to its own full guide with routes, timing and the official pages to check before you go.

Drive times assume normal traffic from downtown. Weekend mornings on westbound I-70 and Sunday afternoons eastbound can add an hour or more, which is why the mountain trips reward an early start.

How to pick the right one

If you have a half day

Golden is the obvious answer. It sits at the base of the foothills just west of the city, so you can walk Clear Creek, visit a museum and be back for dinner downtown. Red Rocks, which we cover in its own Red Rocks guide, pairs naturally with Golden because the two are about fifteen minutes apart.

If you want a hike and a meal in the same place

Boulder does this better than anywhere else near Denver. The trailheads at Chautauqua Park are a short drive or shuttle ride from Pearl Street, so a morning on the Flatiron trails and a late lunch downtown fit comfortably into one day.

If you want to feel the high mountains

Mount Blue Sky and Rocky Mountain National Park both put you above treeline. Mount Blue Sky is closer and more dramatic in a single climb; the national park is bigger, wilder and much better for wildlife. Both are seasonal at their highest points, and both used reservation systems in 2026.

If you are traveling with people who do not hike

Georgetown and Idaho Springs offer a narrow-gauge train, gold mine tours and a hot springs pool, all a few minutes from the interstate. Colorado Springs adds Garden of the Gods, where paved paths wind between red rock fins, and a road or train to the top of Pikes Peak.

Reservations and permits in 2026

Several of Colorado's most popular high places now manage crowds with timed reservations. These rules are revised every year, so treat the details below as a starting point and confirm on the official page before you go.

  • Rocky Mountain National ParkTimed entry ran from May 22 into mid-October 2026, required from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. for most of the park and for a longer daily window on Bear Lake Road.
  • Mount Blue SkyThe scenic byway reopened in late May 2026 after a long repair closure, with Recreation.gov reservations required for daytime driving and parking.
  • Pikes Peak HighwayA low-cost timed-entry permit applied to vehicles passing mile 7 from late May through September 30, 2026, separate from highway admission.
  • Everything else hereGolden, Boulder, Georgetown, Idaho Springs and Garden of the Gods need no entry reservation, though individual tours and attractions sell tickets.

Driving in the mountains: what visitors underestimate

The mountain day trips west of Denver all use Interstate 70, one of the busiest and most weather-exposed highways in the country. A few habits make a big difference.

  1. Leave early. Being through the Morrison and Genesee stretch before 7 a.m. on a weekend usually means a normal drive. Leaving at 9 a.m. often does not.
  2. Check COtrip before you go. The Colorado Department of Transportation's COtrip site shows closures, chain-law notices and live travel times.
  3. Know the traction rules. From September through May, Colorado can activate its traction law on I-70 between Dotsero and Morrison. Passenger vehicles then need adequate all-weather or snow tires, four-wheel or all-wheel drive, or approved traction devices. Rental cars are not automatically compliant, so ask at the counter.
  4. Fill up in the city. Gas and charging are available in the mountain towns but can be sparse on scenic byways.
  5. Respect the altitude. Several of these trips climb from Denver's 5,280 feet to above 12,000. Drink water, eat, and turn around if someone develops a worsening headache or shortness of breath.

Day trips without a car

Denver's transit network reaches farther than many visitors assume. RTD's Flatiron Flyer buses link Union Station with Boulder throughout the day. CDOT runs Bustang intercity coaches, including routes toward Colorado Springs and the I-70 mountain corridor, and has offered seasonal service to Estes Park. In winter, Amtrak's Winter Park Express has run ski trains from Union Station to the base of Winter Park Resort; Amtrak has said the train returns for the 2026 to 2027 season, with dates still to be published. For the high scenic roads, a guided tour is the realistic alternative to driving yourself.

October brings the last good window for the high country. Trail Ridge Road and the upper Mount Blue Sky road usually close for winter in fall and reopen in late spring, while the lower day trips stay open year-round. Our Denver in winter guide covers what still works from November to April.

Combining day trips

A few pairings work especially well. Red Rocks and Golden share the same stretch of foothills, so a sunrise at the amphitheatre followed by breakfast on Golden's Washington Avenue makes an efficient morning. Idaho Springs sits at the bottom of the road to Mount Blue Sky, so a summit morning ends nicely at a hot springs pool. Garden of the Gods and Manitou Springs are next to each other, and both sit near the Pikes Peak Highway and the Cog Railway depot. Trying to fit Rocky Mountain National Park together with anything else in one day is usually a mistake; the park deserves the whole day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest day trip from Denver without a car?

Boulder. RTD's Flatiron Flyer buses run between Denver Union Station and downtown Boulder, and Boulder's Park-to-Park shuttle served Chautauqua on summer weekends in 2026. Most mountain day trips west on I-70 realistically need a car, a tour or CDOT's Bustang service.

Do I need a reservation to visit Rocky Mountain National Park from Denver?

In the 2026 summer season, yes, for most of the day. Timed entry was required from 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. between May 22 and mid-October, with a separate, longer window for the Bear Lake Road corridor. Arriving before or after those hours avoided the permit, but the park entrance fee still applied.

What is the best day trip from Denver in winter?

Georgetown and Idaho Springs work well because they sit right off I-70 and do not depend on high seasonal roads. Garden of the Gods in Colorado Springs is another strong winter choice since it sits far lower than the mountain towns and is free year-round.

How do I avoid I-70 traffic on a mountain day trip?

Leave Denver early, ideally before 7 a.m. on weekends, and plan to start home before early afternoon or after the evening rush. Sunday afternoons eastbound and Friday and Saturday mornings westbound are the slowest. CDOT's COtrip site shows live conditions and travel times.
