A Day Trip to Golden, Colorado

Golden is the closest thing to a mountain town you can reach from downtown Denver in half an hour. A creek runs through the middle of it, the foothills rise straight behind it, and it once served as the capital of Colorado Territory.

Golden manages to feel like a small Western town while sitting inside the Denver metro area. Washington Avenue, the main street, runs down to Clear Creek, where kayakers play in engineered rapids and families wade at the edges on summer afternoons. Behind town, the flat-topped North and South Table Mountains and the steep face of Lookout Mountain frame every view. It makes an easy first taste of the foothills, and a natural pairing with Red Rocks on the same day.

A little history, because it explains the town

Golden grew up in 1859 as a supply town for prospectors heading into the canyons of Clear Creek during the Pikes Peak gold rush. For several years in the 1860s it served as the capital of Colorado Territory before the seat of government moved to Denver. The Colorado School of Mines followed in the 1870s, and a brewery founded beside the creek in the same decade grew into one of the largest in the world. You can still read all three chapters on a single walk: the territorial-era storefronts downtown, the Mines campus on the hill, and the brewery complex along the water.

A simple plan for the day

  1. Morning on Lookout Mountain. Drive the winding Lariat Loop road up Lookout Mountain for the view back over the plains, then visit the grave of William F. "Buffalo Bill" Cody and the museum beside it. The museum's observation deck looks across the Front Range and the plains.
  2. Late morning downtown. Park once and walk. Washington Avenue has independent shops, cafes and the arch that greets visitors to town. The Golden History Museum and Park tells the town's story in a compact space.
  3. Lunch, then the creek. The Clear Creek Trail follows the water right through town. In warm months you will see kayakers in the Clear Creek Whitewater Park and plenty of people cooling their feet at the gravel bars.
  4. Afternoon pick. Choose one: the Mines Museum of Earth Science for minerals and meteorites, the Colorado Railroad Museum for historic locomotives, or a brewery tour if everyone in your group is of legal drinking age.
  5. Golden hour at Red Rocks. If the amphitheatre is not hosting a show, finish with sunset at Red Rocks, roughly fifteen minutes south.

What to see in Golden

Clear Creek and the whitewater park

The creek is the town's centerpiece. A paved and gravel path runs along both banks, connecting parks, a library, the brewery and downtown. In spring the snowmelt turns the water fast and loud, and the whitewater park draws experienced paddlers. By late summer flows drop and the shallows become a neighborhood beach. Treat the water with respect: it stays cold all year, and runoff season can be dangerous even for strong swimmers.

Lookout Mountain and the Buffalo Bill Museum and Grave

Lookout Mountain Park was the first property in the Denver Mountain Parks system, and it is still one of the best viewpoints near the city. Buffalo Bill Cody, the scout and Wild West showman, was buried here in 1917. The grave is a short walk from the museum, and the park follows sunrise-to-sunset style hours. A trail from the museum also reaches the Colorow Point overlook. The road up is narrow with tight switchbacks and is shared with many cyclists, so drive slowly.

Colorado School of Mines and its museum

The Mines campus sits on a rise above downtown, and its Museum of Earth Science holds an outstanding public collection of Colorado minerals, along with gemstones, fossils and mining artifacts. It is a strong rainy-day stop and works well for curious kids.

Colorado Railroad Museum

On the north side of town, this outdoor and indoor museum preserves narrow-gauge and standard-gauge equipment from Colorado's railroad era, with ride days on a published schedule. Check the calendar before you plan around a train ride.

The brewery

Golden's large brewery along Clear Creek offers visitor tours with a tasting for adults. Tour formats, days and age rules change, so book or check times online before driving out.

Outdoors near Golden

Golden is surrounded by public land. North Table Mountain Park, run by Jefferson County Open Space, has a steep climb to a broad mesa top with long views. Golden Gate Canyon State Park lies about a half-hour drive up into the hills northwest of town and offers forest trails, aspen groves and campgrounds; note that the park has no cell service. Clear Creek Canyon, west of town on US-6, is a dramatic drive between rock walls with climbing crags and creekside pullouts.

Jefferson County's foothills trails are shared by hikers, mountain bikers and horses. Uphill traffic has the right of way, dogs must be leashed where posted, and trails get muddy in spring. If a trail is muddy, walking through the mud rather than around it keeps the path from widening.

Getting there and parking

From downtown Denver, US-6 west or I-70 west to the Golden exits takes most drivers under 35 minutes outside rush hour. Downtown Golden has metered street parking and public lots; on summer weekends the lots near the creek fill early. Without a car, RTD's W Line light rail runs from Denver Union Station to the Jefferson County Government Center–Golden station, a short bus or bike ride from downtown Golden.

Make it a longer day

Golden is a gateway rather than an end point. Continue west on I-70 to Idaho Springs and Georgetown, or head north on CO-93 toward Boulder, which is about 25 minutes away. The Denver Mountain Parks guide covers the bison overlook at Genesee and other city-owned parks along the same stretch of foothills.

Frequently asked questions

How far is Golden from Denver?

Golden sits about 15 miles west of downtown Denver. The drive usually takes 25 to 35 minutes, and RTD's W Line light rail also runs from Denver Union Station to the Jefferson County Government Center–Golden station.

Is Golden worth a day trip from Denver?

Yes, especially if you want a mountain-town feel without a long drive. Clear Creek, the historic downtown, Lookout Mountain and the museums fill a relaxed half day or a full day, and Red Rocks is about fifteen minutes away.

Can you go tubing in Clear Creek in Golden?

Tubing is popular in summer, but Clear Creek is cold and fast, especially during spring runoff in May and June. Check current flows and any local advisories before going in, wear a life jacket, and avoid the water when the city or county posts closures.

Is the Buffalo Bill Museum and Grave free?

The grave and the park around it on Lookout Mountain are free to visit, and the museum's observation deck has been free to access. The museum itself has charged admission; check the official site for current prices and hours.
